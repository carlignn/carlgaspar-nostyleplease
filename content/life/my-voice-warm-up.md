I work from home and I barely talk all day. When talking is finally needed, my mouth is cold: I mumble, I can't talk straight. This is the warm-up I run to start my day.

## 1. Wake up the voice (1 minute)

- [ ] **Hum** with lips closed for 20 seconds, until you feel the buzz in your face.
- [ ] **Lip trills** — blow through loose lips like a motorboat. Slide the pitch low to high and back, 3 times.
- [ ] **Siren** — on "ng" (as in *sing*), glide from your lowest note to your highest and back, 2 times.
- [ ] **Big yawn-sigh** — open wide, sigh out on "haaa". Relaxes the throat and jaw.

## 2. Wake up the mouth (tongue twisters)

## 3. Say it back (1 minute)

After reading, close the book and explain what I just read **out loud, in my own words**, for one minute. **No stopping.** If a word won't come within 3 seconds, describe it instead and keep going. Reading aloud trains the mouth. This trains finding the words.

## The minimum

On a low day: **one hum, one twister, one minute of saying it back.** That counts.

## Talking on the other days

- **Voice comms when you game.** If you play Valorant or Dota, talk on comms instead of staying quiet. Fast calls under pressure are very close to real conversation practice.
- **Speak up at work.** Use your mic in meetings, and give a spoken update instead of a chat message when you can. Real stakes, real English, already on your schedule.
- **Talk with me by voice, in short sessions.** Say "give me a question," then answer out loud **without pausing your dictation.** 5 minutes. I'll tell you whether it made sense.
- **Narrate what you're building.** Record a 1-minute voice memo or video explaining today's progress on your game. It's speaking practice, and it's also making something, which you've been wanting to do more of.
- **Language-exchange apps** (HelloTalk, Tandem). You voice-chat with real people learning Tagalog or Filipino while you practice English. With your night shift, people in other time zones are awake when you are.
- **Small talk outside the house.** The guard, the rider, the vendor, teammates after a game. Thirty seconds each.
